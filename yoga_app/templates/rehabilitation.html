1. Rotator Cuff Strain & Impingement Syndrome Shoulder

Condition Overview: Rotator cuff impingement occurs when supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, or subscapularis tendons are compressed under the acromion during arm elevation. Common in overhead athletes and desk workers with poor scapulothoracic rhythm.

Phase 1 – Acute (Wk 1–2) Pendulum swings, passive ROM, ice/heat modalities, rest from overhead loading
Phase 2 – Sub-Acute (Wk 3–5) Scapular retractions, resisted ER/IR, side-lying external rotation
Phase 3 – Strengthening (Wk 6–10) Full rotator cuff circuit, shoulder press technique, sport-specific loading
Key Exercises: Shoulder External Rotation, Scapular Wall Slide, Pendulum Arm Swing, Side Lateral Raise, Reverse Fly
3×12 reps 4–5 weeks Avoid overhead <90°
Stop if: Sharp pinching felt at front of shoulder during elevation, or numbness radiates down arm.
AI Tip: The live pose engine detects elbow flare >38cm from hip — a primary impingement risk factor — and alerts you in real time.
2. Frozen Shoulder (Adhesive Capsulitis) Shoulder

Condition Overview: Adhesive capsulitis causes progressive stiffening of the glenohumeral joint capsule, leading to severe ROM restriction. Progresses through freezing, frozen, and thawing phases over 12–18 months.

Freezing Phase Pain-guided ROM, pendulum exercises, gentle passive stretching within comfort
Frozen Phase Aggressive stretching, joint mobilization, capsular stretches, pulley exercises
Thawing Phase Progressive strengthening, functional movement patterns, overhead loading
Key Exercises: Pendulum Arm Swing, Cross-Body Shoulder Stretch, Overhead Arm Stretch, Chest Opener, Shoulder External Rotation
Stop if: Extreme pain during passive range — frozen shoulder stretches must be at the pain threshold, not beyond.
3. Bicipital Tendinopathy & SLAP Lesion Shoulder

Condition Overview: Bicipital tendinopathy involves inflammation or degeneration of the long head of biceps brachii where it attaches to the superior labrum (SLAP). Common in throwing athletes and overhead lifters.

Key Exercises: Biceps Stretch, Forearm Supination, Pendulum Swing, Scapular Retraction
Eccentric loading protocol 8–12 weeks
Stop if: Clicking with pain at shoulder, or anterior shoulder pain with forearm supination against resistance.
4. Shoulder Instability & AC Joint Sprain Shoulder

Condition Overview: Acromioclavicular (AC) joint sprains range from Grade I (ligament stretch) to Grade III (complete disruption). Scapular stabilization is the cornerstone of rehab to protect the AC joint.

Grade I–II Rest 1–2 weeks, scapular stabilization, progressive strengthening without overhead
Grade III Conservative or surgical management, 3–6 month rehabilitation program
Key Exercises: Scapular Wall Slide, Scapular Retraction, Side Lateral Raise, Reverse Fly
Avoid bench press and overhead pressing until AC pain-free throughout full ROM.
5. Shoulder Bursitis (Subacromial) Shoulder

Condition Overview: Subacromial bursa inflammation causes pain with arm elevation between 60°–120° (painful arc). Often co-exists with rotator cuff tendinopathy.

Management Protocol: Activity modification, anti-inflammatory phase (1–2 weeks), then gradual rotator cuff strengthening. Corticosteroid injection may precede physiotherapy in severe cases.
Key Exercises: Pendulum Arm Swing, External Rotation (sidelying), Scapular Depression exercises, Chest Opener
Avoid: Overhead press, pull-ups, and any activity recreating the painful arc during acute phase.
6. Scapular Dyskinesis & Winging Shoulder

Condition Overview: Abnormal scapular movement during arm elevation, often caused by serratus anterior or lower trapezius weakness. Leads to impingement, rotator cuff overload, and shoulder pain.

Key Exercises: Scapular Wall Slide, Scapular Retraction, Bird-Dog, Push-up Plus, Serratus Activation
AI Tip: Use the Shoulder Rotation assessment — the AI monitors shoulder-height symmetry in real time and alerts if one scapula elevates excessively.
7. Chronic Low Back Pain & Lumbar Disc Disease Spine

Condition Overview: Degenerative lumbar disc disease causes disc bulge/herniation compressing nerve roots, causing radiating leg pain (sciatica), morning stiffness, and position-dependent pain.

Acute Phase McKenzie extension or flexion-biased postures, pain-guided ADLs, avoid sitting >20 min
Sub-Acute Core stabilization, Cat-Cow, Pelvic Tilt, Bird-Dog, gentle walking program
Chronic Phase Progressive loading, deadlift mechanics, return to sport/work specific tasks
Key Exercises: Cat-Cow, Pelvic Tilt Stabilization, Bird-Dog, Dead Bug, Child's Pose, Spinal Arch
Daily 2×10 reps 20–30 min walks
Red Flags: Saddle anaesthesia, bilateral leg symptoms, or bowel/bladder dysfunction — seek emergency care immediately.
8. Sciatica & Piriformis Syndrome Spine

Condition Overview: Sciatic nerve irritation causes burning pain from gluteal region down posterior leg to foot. Piriformis syndrome specifically involves sciatic nerve compression by the piriformis muscle.

Key Exercises: Piriformis Stretch (figure-four), Hip Flexor Lunge, Child's Pose, Hamstring Stretch, Neural Flossing (sciatic nerve mobilization)
30-sec holds × 3 Avoid prolonged sitting
The piriformis stretch in a figure-four position is most effective when the ankle is flexed (dorsiflexed) to maximize external rotation stretch.
Differentiate from lumbar disc herniation — sciatic neural tension tests (SLR, slump test) help confirm piriformis vs. disc origin.
9. Forward Head Posture & Cervical Dysfunction Spine

Condition Overview: Forward head posture (FHP) increases cervical compressive forces by ~4kg per inch of forward displacement. Causes upper trapezius tension, cervicogenic headaches, and thoracic kyphosis.

Key Exercises: Chin Tuck (deep cervical flexion), Neck Roll, Cervical Lateral Flexion, Scapular Retraction, Thoracic Extension
10 reps hourly at desk Monitor height
AI Tip: The AI skeleton detects chin-to-shoulder landmark alignment in real time — the Chin Tuck assessment shows green arrows guiding the correct head retraction path.
10. Thoracic Kyphosis (Hunchback Posture) Spine

Condition Overview: Excessive thoracic flexion (>45° Cobb angle) leading to vertebral wedging, pectoral shortening, and posterior thoracic muscle elongation weakness. Common in adolescents (Scheuermann's) and elderly (osteoporotic).

Key Exercises: Thoracic Extension (foam roller), Cobra Back Extension, Scapular Retraction, Wall Slide, Chest Opener, Bird-Dog
Mobility Thoracic foam rolling daily, 10 reps thoracic extension over roller
Strength Mid-trap and rhomboid exercises, 3×15 face pulls
Retraining Postural re-education, workspace ergonomics, breathing pattern
11. Spondylolisthesis & Facet Joint Pain Spine

Condition Overview: Anterior vertebral slippage (spondylolisthesis) or arthritic facet joint degeneration causing extension-biased back pain, particularly with prolonged standing or lumbar extension activities.

Key Exercises: Pelvic Tilt (posterior), Cat-Cow (flexion bias), Dead Bug, Double-Knee-to-Chest, Hamstring Stretch
Avoid: Lumbar hyperextension, heavy axial loading (barbell squat/deadlift) in high-grade slippage without clearance.
12. Scoliosis Management & Postural Correction Spine

Condition Overview: Lateral spinal curvature with vertebral rotation. Mild idiopathic scoliosis (<25°) responds well to Schroth method physiotherapy emphasizing 3D auto-correction and breathing.

Key Exercises: Side Plank (convex side), Standing Side Bend (concave opening), Thoracic Rotation, Spinal Arch Flexion, Swimming pattern
The AI skeleton monitors bilateral shoulder and hip height in real time — critical for detecting compensatory patterns during scoliosis exercises.
13. Spinal Stenosis & Neurogenic Claudication Spine

Condition Overview: Narrowing of spinal canal causing leg heaviness, bilateral leg pain worsened by standing/walking (claudication). Improved with spinal flexion (sitting, leaning forward on cart).

Key Exercises: Seated Forward Bend, Double Knee-to-Chest, Stationary cycling (flexed posture), Cat-Cow, Aquatic therapy
Avoid: Lumbar extension positions — any exercise that recreates bilateral leg symptoms indicates neural compromise.
14. IT Band Syndrome (ITBS) Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Friction syndrome at the lateral femoral epicondyle from repetitive knee flexion/extension in runners and cyclists. Primary cause is gluteus medius weakness leading to excessive hip adduction and knee internal rotation.

Key Exercises: Clamshell Hip Abduction, Monster Walk Lateral Band, Standing Side Bend (IT band stretch), High Knee Lift, Single Leg Squat
Foam Rolling Protocol: TFL and lateral quadriceps rolling 2×90s before exercise, avoiding direct pressure on lateral knee (the compression zone).
Reduce running volume by 50% during acute phase. Return to running only when single-leg squat shows no hip drop.
15. Hip Flexor Tightness & Psoas Syndrome Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Chronic sitting shortens iliopsoas, causing anterior pelvic tilt, lumbar hyperlordosis, and reduced hip extension. Psoas impingement can mimic hip joint pathology.

Key Exercises: Hip Flexor Lunge Stretch (kneeling), Pigeon Pose Variation, Bridge to stretch hip flexors, Cat-Cow posterior pelvic tilt
Acute Kneeling lunge 30s holds, 3× daily
Strength Hip extension strengthening (glutes/hamstrings) to balance
Function Gait retraining, hip extension during walking
16. Knee Osteoarthritis & Patellofemoral Pain Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Patellofemoral pain syndrome (PFPS) involves pain behind/around kneecap during squatting, stairs, and prolonged sitting. Caused by quadriceps imbalance (VMO weakness), hip abductor weakness, and tight lateral structures.

Key Exercises: Terminal Knee Extension, Straight Leg Raise, Wall Sit, Clamshell, Step-Up, Quad Stretch, Calf Raise
Key Metrics: Knee over toes ✓ No valgus collapse VMO contraction check
AI Tip: The Squat assessment detects knee valgus (inward cave) in real time with a RED CROSS joint alert on the knee, and GREEN arrows showing the correct path.
17. ACL Rehabilitation Protocol Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction rehabilitation follows a phased return-to-sport protocol over 9–12 months, prioritizing graft protection, quadriceps re-activation, and proprioceptive retraining.

Phase 1 (0–6w) Graft protection, SLR, quad sets, terminal knee extension, full extension ROM
Phase 2 (6–14w) Closed chain exercises, single-leg squat, lateral band walks, bike
Phase 3 (14–24w) Plyometrics, hop tests, agility drills, sport-specific training
Phase 4 (24w+) Return-to-sport criteria: >90% LSI, negative pivot shift, psychological readiness
Key Exercises: Straight Leg Raise, Quad Set, Wall Sit, Calf Raise, Monster Walk, High Knee Lift, Single Leg Bridge
18. Achilles Tendinopathy & Calf Injury Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Midportion or insertional Achilles tendinopathy from excessive mechanical load. Heavy slow resistance (HSR) eccentric protocol is gold standard for tendon remodelling.

Key Exercises: Eccentric Calf Raise (heel drop off step), Bilateral Calf Raise, Ankle Circles, Soleus Stretch, Tibialis Anterior activation
Alfredson Protocol: 3×15 eccentric heel drops Twice daily 12 weeks minimum
Avoid: NSAIDS long-term (interfere with collagen synthesis), complete rest is counterproductive — load the tendon progressively.
19. Plantar Fasciitis & Heel Pain Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Plantar fasciopathy presents with first-step morning pain at calcaneal insertion. Caused by repetitive tensile loading on plantar fascia, calf tightness, and high-arch or flat-foot biomechanics.

Key Exercises: Calf Stretch (standing & seated), Intrinsic Foot Muscle activation (towel scrunches), Ankle Circles, High Arch formation
Self-Management: Frozen water bottle rolling under arch 5 min morning & evening; night splint to maintain dorsiflexion during sleep.
20. Hip Osteoarthritis & Greater Trochanteric Pain Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Hip OA causes groin or anterior hip pain with rotation; Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome (GTPS) involves gluteal tendinopathy and TFL tightness causing lateral hip pain.

Key Exercises — Hip OA: Clamshell, Glute Bridge, Squat (parallel only), Side-Lying Hip Abduction, Adductor Squeeze
Key Exercises — GTPS: Isometric hip abduction (wall), Clamshell, avoid cross-legged sitting and hip adduction stretches that compress tendon
For GTPS: Avoid all adduction stretches (pigeon pose, crossing legs) — tendon compression worsens symptoms. Load the tendon in abduction instead.
21. Hamstring Strain Rehabilitation Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Proximal hamstring tendinopathy or muscle-tendon junction tears from sprinting. Nordic hamstring curl (eccentric) is evidence-based prevention and rehabilitation.

Grade I (Mild) Active rest, isometrics, begin eccentric in 5–7 days
Grade II (Moderate) 2–4 weeks, progressive eccentric, neuromuscular control drills
Grade III (Severe) 6–12 weeks, surgical consult for complete ruptures, slow progressive loading
Key Exercises: Hamstring Stretch, Nordic Curl, Single Leg Bridge, Dead Bug, Good Morning (light)
22. Hip Impingement (FAI — Femoroacetabular) Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Cam (femoral) or pincer (acetabular) morphology causes labral impingement with deep hip flexion and internal rotation. Common in active young adults — often misdiagnosed as groin strain.

Key Exercises: Piriformis Stretch, Hip Flexor Lunge (controlled depth), Clamshell, Glute Bridge, Side-Lying Hip Abduction
Avoid: Deep squat past 90°, seated hip flexion past 90°, and high-impact activities until cleared by imaging.
23. Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS) Wrist & Hand

Condition Overview: Median nerve compression at carpal tunnel causing nocturnal paraesthesia in thumb, index, middle, and radial half of ring finger. Aggravated by sustained wrist flexion and repetitive hand use.

Conservative Protocol: Neutral wrist splinting (nocturnal), tendon gliding exercises, nerve flossing, ergonomic modification
Key Exercises: Tendon Glides (5 positions), Wrist Extension Stretch, Wrist Flexion Stretch, Median Nerve Floss, Finger Spread
Ergonomics: Mouse pad wrist rest Neutral wrist position Break every 30 min
24. Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylalgia) Wrist & Hand

Condition Overview: Extensor carpi radialis brevis tendinopathy at lateral epicondyle from repetitive wrist extension or gripping. Classic point tenderness 1cm distal to lateral epicondyle.

Key Exercises: Eccentric Wrist Extension (Tyler Twist protocol), Wrist Extension Stretch, Pronation & Supination, Grip Strengthening (pain-free range)
Tyler Twist Protocol: 3×15 reps Eccentric only Daily × 6 weeks Rubber bar device
25. Golfer's Elbow (Medial Epicondylalgia) Wrist & Hand

Condition Overview: Flexor-pronator mass tendinopathy at medial epicondyle causing medial elbow pain with wrist flexion and pronation. Often accompanies ulnar nerve irritation (cubital tunnel).

Key Exercises: Eccentric Wrist Flexion, Wrist Flexion Stretch, Pronation & Supination, Forearm Flexion, Finger Tendon Glides
Differentiate from ulnar nerve: check for small finger/ring finger tingling which indicates cubital tunnel syndrome — requires ulnar nerve mobilization.
26. De Quervain's Tenosynovitis (Thumb Tendon) Wrist & Hand

Condition Overview: Stenosing tenosynovitis of APL and EPB tendons at radial styloid. Finkelstein test positive. Common in new mothers and mobile phone users (repetitive thumb scrolling).

Key Exercises: Thumb Opposition, Radial Nerve Floss, Wrist Extension (avoiding thumb-in-fist position), Ice massage to first dorsal compartment
27. Trigger Finger & Dupuytren's Contracture Wrist & Hand

Condition Overview: Trigger finger (stenosing flexor tenosynovitis) causes a painful snap when flexing/extending a finger. Dupuytren's causes palmar fibromatosis pulling fingers into flexion.

Key Exercises: Finger Tendon Glides (5-position sequence), Finger Extension Stretches, Intrinsic muscle strengthening, Web-space stretching
28. Wrist Sprain & TFCC Injury Rehab Wrist & Hand

Condition Overview: Triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) tears from FOOSH injury or rotational loading cause ulnar-sided wrist pain. Radioulnar instability testing confirms diagnosis.

Acute (0–3w) Splinting in neutral, pain-free ROM, no pronation/supination
Sub-Acute (3–6w) Progressive forearm rotation, grip work, wrist stability exercises
Return (6–12w) Load-bearing, impact activities, sport-specific gripping
29. Core Dysfunction & Abdominal Separation (Diastasis Recti) Core

Condition Overview: Inter-recti distance >2.5cm indicating linea alba dysfunction. Common post-partum — requires transversus abdominis re-education before any traditional abdominal exercises.

Key Exercises: Transversus Abdominis Activation (TvA), Diaphragmatic Breathing, Dead Bug (gentle), Pelvic Floor Contraction, Bird-Dog (modified)
Avoid: Traditional crunches, sit-ups, oblique twists, and heavy lifting until IRD is <2cm and linea alba tension is restored.
30. Core Stabilization for Chronic Back Pain Core

Condition Overview: Motor control deficits in multifidus and TvA activation timing are linked to recurrent low back pain. Specific stabilization exercises (SSE) retrain the deep intrinsic stabilizer system.

Key Exercises: Plank Core Hold, Dead Bug, Bird-Dog, Pelvic Tilt, Side Plank, Glute Bridge
Progressive overload weekly
AI Tip: The Plank assessment monitors hip sagging in real time — a common form fault that shifts load from core to lumbar extensors, increasing injury risk.
31. Pelvic Floor Dysfunction & Urinary Incontinence Core

Condition Overview: Pelvic floor weakness (hypotonic) or overactivity (hypertonic) causing stress or urge urinary incontinence. Kegel exercises are first-line — but only effective for hypotonic dysfunction.

Key Exercises (Hypotonic): Kegel Contractions (fast + slow), Pelvic Bridge, Deep Squat with lift, Core & Diaphragm coordination
Key Exercises (Hypertonic): Pelvic floor drops (reverse Kegel), Diaphragmatic breathing, Hip Flexor Release, Child's Pose
32. Breathing Pattern Disorders & Respiratory Rehab Core

Condition Overview: Paradoxical breathing (apical/chest-dominant) reduces diaphragmatic excursion, increases thoracic tension, and elevates cortisol. Impacts core pressure management and spinal stability.

Key Exercises: 360° Diaphragmatic Breathing, Box Breathing (4-4-4-4), Crocodile Breathing (prone), Intraabdominal Pressure training, Yoga Nidra breathing
33. Stroke Rehabilitation & Neurological Recovery Neuro & Balance

Condition Overview: Post-stroke neuroplasticity relies on task-specific repetitive movement to rewire neural pathways. AI pose detection enables objective assessment of movement quality during neurological rehabilitation.

Early (0–3 mo) Bed mobility, transfers, sitting balance, basic ADL retraining
Sub-Acute (3–6 mo) Gait training, arm reach tasks, constraint-induced movement therapy
Chronic (>6 mo) Intensive task practice, virtual reality, robotic assist, continued gains possible
Key Exercises: Weight shifting, Arm Reach & Grasp, Shoulder Rotation (affected side), Core Sitting Balance, Ankle Dorsiflexion
34. Fall Prevention & Balance Rehabilitation Neuro & Balance

Condition Overview: Falls are the leading cause of injury in adults >65. Multifactorial — vestibular, proprioceptive, visual, and muscular deficits all contribute. Otago Exercise Programme is evidence-based.

Key Exercises: Tree Pose Balance, High Knee Lift, Single-Leg Stand, Tandem Walking, Calf Raise, Backward Walking, Stepping patterns
Otago Programme (home-based): 3× weekly ≥6 months 40% fall reduction
AI Tip: The Tree Pose Balance assessment tracks centre-of-mass sway through hip landmark analysis — quantifying balance improvement over sessions.
35. Parkinson's Disease Exercise Therapy Neuro & Balance

Condition Overview: Exercise modifies Parkinson's disease progression through dopaminergic neuroprotection. High-intensity aerobic exercise (HIIT), boxing, and tango dancing show superior outcomes vs. standard PT.

Key Exercises: Large-amplitude movement training (LSVT BIG), Boxing-inspired punches, Tango rhythm stepping, Balance challenge, High-step marching
36. Vestibular Rehabilitation & BPPV Neuro & Balance

Condition Overview: Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) from loose otoconia in semicircular canals — most common cause of vertigo. Epley maneuver has 80% single-treatment success rate.

Epley Maneuver: Series of 4 head positions held 30s each, designed to reposition displaced otoconia from posterior canal
VRT Exercises: Gaze stabilization, Habituation exercises, Balance retraining, Brandt-Daroff exercises
37. Concussion & Post-Concussion Syndrome Rehab Neuro & Balance

Condition Overview: Sub-symptom threshold aerobic exercise accelerates concussion recovery — Zurich Consensus Protocol governs return-to-play stages. AI movement analysis helps monitor gait & balance recovery objectively.

Stage 1 Rest until asymptomatic (24–48h), then sub-threshold aerobic activity
Stage 2–3 Sport-specific exercise without contact, complex movement patterns
Stage 4–5 Non-contact drills, full practice, medical clearance, return to competition
Key Exercises: Tandem Walking, Balance Board, Visual tracking, Cervical stabilization, Neck strengthening
38. Ankle Sprain Rehabilitation (Lateral & High) Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: ATFL/CFL lateral ligament sprains are the most common sports injury. PEACE & LOVE protocol replaces RICE — Protection, Elevation, Avoid anti-inflammatory, Compression, Education, then Load, Optimism, Vascularisation, Exercise.

Phase 1 (0–3d) PEACE — protect, elevate, avoid NSAIDS, compress, education
Phase 2 (3–14d) LOVE — progressive weight bearing, balance training, ROM restoration
Phase 3 (2–6w) Proprioceptive retraining, agility, return-to-sport criteria
Key Exercises: Ankle Circles, Calf Raise (progressive), Single Leg Balance, Wobble Board, Hop & Stick
39. Thrower's Shoulder & GIRD (Internal Rotation Deficit) Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: Glenohumeral internal rotation deficit (GIRD) is >18° loss in throwing athletes from posterior capsule tightening. Creates abnormal humeral head translation and labral stress during deceleration phase.

Key Exercises: Sleeper Stretch (posterior capsule), Cross-Body Stretch, ER strengthening, Rotator cuff eccentric, Scapular position correction
The Shoulder External Rotation assessment monitors shoulder horizontal alignment — key for identifying asymmetrical GIRD patterns.
40. Cyclist's Injuries & Bike Fit Rehabilitation Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: Cycling injuries include knee pain (PFPS, ITBS), lower back pain (flexed posture), neck/upper trapezius overload, and saddle-related perineal pressure. Bike fit is the cornerstone of prevention.

Key Exercises: Hip Flexor Lunge, Piriformis Stretch, Glute Bridge, Thoracic Extension, Neck Roll, Hamstring Stretch, Quadriceps Stretch
41. Swimmer's Shoulder & Overuse Rehab Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: Shoulder impingement & rotator cuff strain from high-volume overhead freestyle swimming. Mid-pull hand crossover and dropped elbow during catch are primary biomechanical faults.

Key Exercises: Shoulder External Rotation, Scapular Retraction, Cross-Body Stretch, Triceps Stretch, Core Plank, Lat Pull-Through
42. Runner's Knee & Tibial Stress Fracture Prevention Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: Runner's knee (PFPS) and medial tibial stress syndrome (shin splints) are load-related injuries from training volume errors. The 10% rule — increasing mileage no faster than 10% per week — is essential prevention.

Key Exercises: Calf Raise (bilateral & single-leg), Hip Abductor strengthening, Foot Arch activation, Anterior Tibialis activation, High Knee Lift
43. Returning to Sport Post-Surgery Protocol Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: Return-to-sport (RTS) decisions require biological healing, neuromuscular function, physical capacity, and psychological readiness. AI pose analysis provides objective functional metrics to guide RTS criteria.

RTS Criteria Matrix: Limb Symmetry Index >90% Single-leg hop test ✓ Pain ≤2/10 VAS Psychological readiness scale ≥95
44. Cervical Radiculopathy (Pinched Neck Nerve) Spine

Condition Overview: Cervical disc herniation or foraminal stenosis causing nerve root compression — arm pain, weakness, and paraesthesia in a dermatomal pattern. C5-C6 and C6-C7 levels most commonly affected.

Key Exercises: Chin Tuck, Neural Tension Release, Cervical Lateral Flexion (away from pain), Scapular Depression, Postural correction
Avoid: Cervical extension/rotation toward symptomatic side
45. Post-Mastectomy & Breast Cancer Rehab Shoulder

Condition Overview: Post-mastectomy patients experience cording (axillary web syndrome), lymphoedema, and shoulder ROM deficits. Progressive ROM exercises are introduced 2–4 weeks post-surgery based on wound healing.

Key Exercises: Pendulum Arm Swing, Progressive shoulder flexion (wall walk), Arm circles, Scapular mobility, Lymphatic drainage techniques
Avoid: Heavy lifting (>2kg) on affected side, blood pressure cuff, injections on affected arm. Protect lymphoedema limb from infection.
46. Prenatal & Postnatal Exercise Physiotherapy Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Pregnancy-related pelvic girdle pain (PGP), pubic symphysis diastasis, and sacroiliac joint dysfunction require modified exercises maintaining pelvic girdle stability throughout all trimesters.

Safe Exercises (All Trimesters): Pelvic Floor contractions, Cat-Cow (modified), Glute Bridge, Clam, Side-lying exercises, Swimming, Walking
Avoid: Supine position after 16 weeks, heavy loading, high-impact activities, exercises causing pubic pain.
47. Cardiac Rehabilitation Exercise Protocol Core

Condition Overview: Post-myocardial infarction or cardiac surgery rehabilitation uses structured aerobic and resistance training to improve cardiovascular fitness, reduce mortality, and return to functional independence.

Phase I (Hospital) Seated/standing activity, self-care ADLs, education
Phase II (Outpatient) Supervised monitored exercise, ECG-guided intensity, 36 sessions
Phase III (Maintenance) Independent exercise program, aerobic + resistance training
48. Multiple Sclerosis (MS) Exercise Therapy Neuro & Balance

Condition Overview: Aerobic and resistance training is beneficial for MS — improves fatigue, strength, balance, and quality of life without exacerbating the condition (Uhthoff's phenomenon: exercise-induced heat sensitivity, not worsening).

Key Exercises: Seated strength training, Balance exercises, Cooling vests during aerobic work, Yoga for relaxation, Progressive mobility training
49. Total Hip & Knee Replacement Rehabilitation Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Total joint replacement rehab focuses on early mobilization to prevent DVT, achieve functional ROM (>90° knee flexion, 0° extension), and restore quadriceps strength for return to daily activities by 6–12 weeks.

Post-Op Day 1 Bed-to-chair transfers, quad sets, SLR, ankle pumps
Week 1–3 Progressive gait training, ROM exercises, step training
Week 6–12 Stair climbing, stationary bike, return to driving, community walking
THR Precautions: No hip flexion >90°, no internal rotation, no cross-leg sitting for 6–12 weeks.
50. Occupational Health & Repetitive Strain Prevention Shoulder

Condition Overview: Work-related musculoskeletal disorders (WRMSDs) from repetitive movement, sustained awkward postures, and heavy lifting affect 1 in 3 workers. Prevention through ergonomic design and microbreaks is most effective.

Key Interventions: Workstation ergonomics assessment, Microbreak exercises (5 min/hour), Shoulder shrugs, Wrist Extension stretches, Neck Roll, Thoracic Rotation, Postural reset program
Desk Worker Protocol (Every 60 min): 5× Chin Tucks 5× Thoracic Rotations 5× Wrist Circles Stand & Walk 2 min
51. Overtraining Syndrome & Recovery Optimization Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: Overtraining syndrome (OTS) presents as performance decline, fatigue, mood disturbance, and increased injury rate from chronic training load exceeding recovery capacity. HRV monitoring guides return to training.

Recovery Protocol: Mandatory rest 2–4 weeks, sleep optimization, nutritional support (protein 1.6–2g/kg), gentle yoga, stress management, progressive return guided by HRV data
52. Sleep-Related Musculoskeletal Pain & Recovery Core

Condition Overview: Poor sleep quality elevates inflammatory markers, increases central sensitization, and reduces pain thresholds. 7–9 hours of sleep reduces musculoskeletal injury risk by 60% in athletes.

Therapeutic Exercise: Yoga Nidra (body scan relaxation), Restorative Yoga before bed, Diaphragmatic breathing, Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR), Gentle Cat-Cow
53. Fibromyalgia & Chronic Pain Syndrome Management Neuro & Balance

Condition Overview: Fibromyalgia involves central sensitization with widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, and cognitive dysfunction. Aerobic exercise (aquatic therapy, walking, cycling) is the most evidence-based treatment — superior to pharmacology.

Pacing Principle: Start below symptom threshold — 5–10 min walking — and increase by 10% weekly. Avoid the boom-bust cycle of overexertion followed by prolonged flare.
Key Exercises: Gentle Yoga, Tai Chi, Aquatic therapy, Warm water walking, Breathing exercises, Progressive relaxation
54. Osteoporosis & Bone Health Exercise Protocol Hip & Leg

Condition Overview: Weight-bearing and resistance exercise stimulates bone mineral density through mechano-transduction. High-impact activities (jumping, hopping) combined with resistance training provide greatest osteogenic stimulus.

Key Exercises: Calf Raise (impact loading), Squats, Glute Bridge, Resistance band exercises, Walking, Heel drops
Avoid: Spinal flexion under load, high-fall-risk activities, exercises recreating vertebral fracture mechanism (forward bending with weight).
55. Youth Athlete Growth & Apophysitis Prevention Sports Rehab

Condition Overview: Osgood-Schlatter (tibial tuberosity), Sever's (calcaneal apophysis), and Little Leaguer's shoulder (proximal humeral apophysitis) are growth-plate stress injuries in adolescent athletes during peak growth velocity.

Key Exercises: Quad Stretch, Hamstring Stretch, Calf Stretch, Reduced-impact training modifications, Swimming as cross-training, Core strengthening
During Peak Growth Velocity (girls 11–13y, boys 13–15y), reduce training load by 20–30% and monitor apophyseal pain closely.
